## Why

The platform ships a single emission-factor catalogue (DEFRA 2025) and applies it to footprints of any year. Declare a 2024 footprint and it is still computed with 2025 factors; nothing in the schema can date a factor.

CYCLO, the verifier, found this in a project feedback session: _"tienen la del DEFRA 2025 y es la única base que está; si yo quisiera declarar una huella del 2024, igual me va a utilizar [la 2025]"_. Their principle is not that newer data is better measured — it is that reality changed: _"un camión que se movió el 2025 es distinto a los camiones que se movieron el 2024, el 2023, el 2022"_, because European and Chilean efficiency norms tighten over time. They also rejected offering a dropdown of other years: _"si existe el factor de emisión de 2025 y tú usas el de 2023, te lo voy a rechazar. Que tengas esa opción no te aporta valor porque los otros años no los debes usar"_.

Normative backing:

- **GHG Protocol** (Corporate Standard, Base Year Adjustments, Scope 2 Guidance): temporal representativeness — use the factor appropriate to the reported period, not the most recent available. Method consistency over time, with transparent documentation of any factor or methodology change. Base-year recalculation policy when an updated factor or GWP crosses the significance threshold.
- **ISO 14064-1:2018**: select factors appropriate to the source, of recognized origin, documented and reproducible; report which were used and where they came from. Principles of relevance, completeness, consistency, accuracy and transparency.
- **ISO 14067 / 14044**: data quality with explicit temporal, geographic and technological representativeness. This is what drives the column being mandatory: an undated factor asserts _"unknown"_, not _"time-invariant"_, and the standard asks for that distinction to be explicit, with deviations documented rather than silenced.
- **ISO 14064-3**: the verifier must be able to trace which factor was applied to each line and judge whether it corresponded to the period. Today that judgment cannot be made from the data at all.

Competitive note: the Huella Chile programme uses a single database for every year. This puts the platform ahead.

## What Changes

- **Add `emission_factor.year` as a required column** — the only schema change. Every factor states which footprint year it is valid for. There is no undated factor: an administrator who believes an IPCC default still applies to 2026 has to say so, which is the assertion a verifier can then check. `source` stays a free-text name carrying the edition (`"DEFRA 2025"`, `"IPCC"`), so name and validity answer different questions.
- **Backfill the existing catalogue to 2025.** All 284 seeded factors become `year = 2025` — the year this catalogue serves. 195 of them already say `DEFRA 2025` in their source, and the remaining ones (83 `IPCC`, 3 `EcoAct 2020`, 3 `Kool, A.`) keep their edition in the name while stating 2025 as their validity. The migration adds the column nullable, backfills, then sets `NOT NULL`.
- **Filter the capture selector** to the footprint's year and nothing else. The filter runs in the Prisma `where` of `getCarbonInventoryMethodology`, before `generateConvertedEmissionFactors` expands each factor across compatible rate units.
- **Reconcile the year server-side in `syncCarbonInventoryLines`.** Filtering is not enforcing: a stale client payload would otherwise write a factor from another year and the filter would never notice. A line whose factor is of another year is saved without that factor — keeping its quantity, unit and everything else — and the response says which lines were left needing one, instead of rejecting a save the user cannot fix. A year change interleaving with a line save is left open rather than locked: it needs two people editing the same footprint at once, and the reconciliation corrects it on the next save.
- **Add the year to the maintainer**: a required field in the emission-factor form and a column in the grid. The dropdown offers `[currentYear - 4 .. currentYear + 1]`; the shared Zod schema carries only a wide static bound, so a factor does not become uneditable because the sliding window moved past it.
- **Clear the stale factors on a year change**: editing a footprint's year clears the frozen factor and result of every line that used a catalogue factor — active and parked alike — after a confirmation modal in step 1. Lines keep their subcategory, dimensions, unit and quantity, and simply ask for a factor again. Lines with a manual factor are kept, because their value and source were typed by the user and no catalogue can restore them.
- **Apply that same clearing to the existing data, in the migration — to every footprint.** Dating the catalogue does not retroactively fix the footprints that used it: one of any year other than the catalogue's keeps a total computed from factors it is no longer offered. The migration spares nothing, submitted and verified footprints included. That erases the record of what was declared, which is what GHG Protocol and ISO 14064-1 ask to preserve, and is admissible only because nothing on the platform was declared in earnest — the only two verification recognitions belong to test organizations. The migration says so in a comment so it is not read as precedent, and it runs behind a `pg_dump`. In exchange, no footprint is left holding a factor of another year, which is what makes the two re-entry routes — duplicating a historical footprint, or returning one with observations — need no code at all.
- **Stop a line from losing its factor identity when it is edited.** `useEmissionCaptureData` currently reloads every line with `baseFactorId: null`, so saving any edit writes a snapshot with no `emissionFactorId`. That breaks both guarantees above through an ordinary edit, and it is already a live defect: the verifier's report derives the gas breakdown and the row identity from that relation, so an edited line loses its breakdown today. The fix reaches the API, not only the hook — `mapLineToResponse` never returned the factor's id, so there was nothing to preserve it from.
- **Carry the year through** methodology duplication and both methodology exports. The per-footprint factor report the verifier reads carries no year of its own — it is scoped to one footprint, so the year is constant across its rows — but it does switch from reading the live `emission_factor.source` to the frozen `applied_factor_source`.

**This does break the API contract**: creating an emission factor now requires a field that did not exist, and the line response gains the factor's id. Because the column is `NOT NULL` with no default, the previous API version cannot insert into it — migrations run before containers are replaced, so creating a factor from the maintainer fails until the new container is up, and during those minutes that container's `sync` still accepts a factor of any year and can write back what the migration just cleared. A maintenance window would close both at no cost, given there are no real users; it was considered and not taken. What is accepted instead: creation failures are admin-only, visible and retryable, and anything written back is reconciled on the next save of that subcategory. A verification query after the deployment lists any line still holding a factor of another year, so the residue that nobody saves again is visible rather than hypothetical. No column default is added, since a default would let a factor be created without stating its year.

## Impact

- **Database**: one required column and one migration — add the column nullable, backfill to 2025, set `NOT NULL`, then clear the catalogue-factor snapshots and results of every footprint whose year differs from the catalogue's, whatever its state, acting on each line's active input so the superseded versions stay as audit trail. The existing partial unique index `emission_factor_unique_subcategory_dims_source` is recreated with `year` as a fifth column; because the column is `NOT NULL`, Postgres' default `NULLS DISTINCT` behaviour is not in play and no `COALESCE` wrapper is needed. No column is added to `carbon_inventory_line_factor`: the clearing, the sync reconciliation and the identity fix together keep a catalogue line on its footprint's year, so it is derived rather than frozen.
- **API write path**: `createEmissionFactor`, `updateEmissionFactor`, `checkDuplicateEmissionFactor`, `validateSourceConsistency`, `syncCarbonInventoryLines`, `updateCarbonInventory`. `duplicateCarbonInventory` and `reviewSubmission` are deliberately left alone — after the migration neither has a source of mismatched data.
- **API read path**: `getCarbonInventoryMethodology`, `getAllEmissionFactors`, `getMethodologyExport` (and therefore `getCarbonInventoryMethodologyExport`, which re-uses the same select, mapper and response schema), `getEmissionFactors`, `cloneEmissionFactors`, and `mapLineToResponse`, which starts returning the line's `emissionFactorId`.
- **Web**: maintainer emission-factor form and columns — including showing a year that has fallen outside the offered window — and business-profiling step 1, whose confirmation lives in `useBusinessProfilingSubmit` so that both exits that save the year are covered. The methodology query key gains the attribute-update token so changing the year actually refetches the year-filtered catalogue — today `carbonInventoryKeys.methodology(id)` omits it, so `useUpdateCarbonInventory`'s invalidation predicate never matches it. That mutation also starts invalidating `EmissionsUpdateDependency` when the payload carries a year, since clearing results invalidates the emissions summary, the rankings, the factor report and the reduction plan, none of which it touches today.
- **Seed**: every factor in the methodology dataset gains a year. This satisfies the requirement that the base catalogue be born dated.
- **Operational consequence**: with no undated factors, the whole catalogue has to be restated every year — the ~195 DEFRA rows change anyway, but the ~86 IPCC and academic ones now also need an explicit yearly assertion. This is the intended cost of making the claim conscious, and it is what turns the deferred bulk import from a convenience into the thing that makes the annual cycle viable.
- **Disruption to expect on deploying**: any footprint whose year is not 2025 is cleared and finds no catalogue for its year, so it falls back to a manual factor. That includes footprints of 2026, the year in progress. Those footprints are today computed with 2025 factors, which is the defect this change exists to remove, and a documented manual factor is the path the verifier prescribed — but the gap stays open until the follow-up PR loads the 2026 set. Count what will be cleared, by year and by state, before rolling out.

**Out of scope**, each deliberately deferred with a TODO in the code:

- Bulk loading a year's set (~284 factors, today one grid row at a time). The drip of a half-loaded set into capture is an accepted risk.
- A year filter on the maintainer grid, which only starts hurting once a second year exists — the same moment the bulk import is needed, and a safer thing to build together than to bolt onto a grid whose rows are addressed by field-array index.
- A draft/active state on the factor, to decouple loading from publishing.
- More than one source per `(subcategory, year)`.
- Freezing the gas breakdown in the snapshot; the verifier report keeps reading it live, as a documented exception.
- Any immutability guard or admin warning when editing factors of an active methodology. Explicitly rejected: the admin is responsible for those changes and understands the risk.
- Ranking by year proximity, per-year methodology versions, new catalogue/set tables, or re-resolving a line's factor when the year changes.
- The 2026 factor set. With the year mandatory and the catalogue dated 2025, a footprint of the current year has no factors until it exists. It comes in a follow-up PR, most naturally as seed data plus a script, which sidesteps the deferred bulk import instead of typing 284 rows into the grid.
